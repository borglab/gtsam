Kalman Filtering and Smoothing by Elimination

The Kalman filter is a staple of robotics state estimation, usually taught through matrix equations for prediction and Kalman gain, yet its structure is far more intuitive when viewed as variable elimination on a factor graph. This interactive companion to KalmanFilteringAndSmoothing.ipynb uses the exact GTSAM and NumPy data from the notebook to show how one graphical operation unifies filtering, fixed-lag smoothing, and nonlinear estimation.

1. Kalman filter = two elimination steps Eliminate xk−1 to form the predictive prior on xk; eliminate xk to fuse it with zk.
2. Fixed-lag smoother = more states kept Keep older Bayes-net conditionals intact, run the same two eliminations at the newest end, and drop the oldest leaf.
3. Nonlinearity = linearize once or repeatedly Linearize factors once at the prediction (EKF), iterate on the current update (IEKF), or relinearize the active window (nonlinear FLS).

1. A Kalman Filter Is Two Elimination Steps

Viewed as a factor graph, the textbook predict–update matrix equations reduce to two sequential variable-elimination steps on a two-state fragment of 2D Point2 positions. Stage 1 eliminates xk−1, leaving two unary factors on xk: the predictive prior p− and the measurement zk. Stage 2 eliminates xk, fusing those two factors into the updated posterior p(xk | z1:k)—the exact Kalman gain update.

2. A Fixed-Lag Smoother Just Keeps More States

Extending a Kalman filter to a fixed-lag smoother requires no new matrix derivations—only keeping a longer chain Bayes net before dropping the oldest leaf. In steady state, Stage 0 liquifies only the newest root p(xk−1) into a factor and attaches u and zk; Stage 1 eliminates xk−1 (leaving p− and zk on xk); and Stage 2 eliminates xk to update the root and drops the oldest leaf xk−4.

Stage 0: Keep Bayes net; liquify root & add u, zkLiquify
Stage 1: Eliminate xk−1 → two factors on xkPredict
Stage 2: Eliminate xk → update root & drop oldest leafUpdate + Drop

3. Nonlinearity: Linearize Factors Once or Several Times

Real robotics sensors such as range beacons are nonlinear, so the factor graph must be linearized around an operating point before elimination. An EKF linearizes each range factor once at the predicted mean, an Iterated EKF relinearizes it several times against that same prediction, and GTSAM's Batch / Incremental Fixed-Lag Smoothers relinearize both motion and range factors together across the 4-state window.
